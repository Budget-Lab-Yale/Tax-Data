# Review of reports 09, 10 and 11

Reviewed, as they stood on 7 October 2026:

- `research/rebuild/background/09_imputation_methods.md`;
- `research/rebuild/background/10_evaluation_design.md`;
- `research/rebuild/background/11_borrowing_change_over_time.md`, with its
  scripts and outputs in `/nfs/roberts/scratch/pi_nrs36/jar335/borrow_change_tmp/`.

How the review was done:

- **Report 11.** I read its scripts. I then re-read the CPS ASEC files with
  my own code and recomputed its headline numbers from the raw files, the SOI
  panel and the saved SSA pages. I also computed sampling errors from the
  Census replicate weights, and re-ran the earner comparison with the
  pandemic-adjusted weights that are on disk.
- **Report 09.** I checked the imputation inventory against the code at
  `ba48f97`. I checked the package claims against the CRAN sources of
  `quantregForest` 1.3-7.1, `ranger` 0.16.0, `randomForest` 4.7-1.1 and
  4.7-1.2, and `drf` 1.3.1 (the versions installed here). Two small Slurm
  tests ran the installed packages.
- **Report 10.** Checked against Tax-Simulator, tax-simulator-gpu and the data
  store. A helper agent did most of this; I re-checked its two main findings.
- **Citations.** A helper agent checked 30 citations through Crossref, arXiv,
  JMLR, NBER, Census and the Realtime Inequality PDF.

All computation ran in Slurm jobs. Scripts and logs are in
`/nfs/roberts/scratch/pi_nrs36/jar335/review_09_11/`. I did not edit any
report. The three bugs already confirmed in report 09 (`state_local_tax.R`
line 176, the consumption bootstrap, the auto-loan `max_ot` cap) were not
re-checked.

A **standard error** below is the usual measure of sampling noise: roughly
two-thirds of repeated samples would land within one standard error of the
estimate.

I found no invented sources and no invented numbers. Every number in report
11 that I recomputed came out the same. The problems are in what the numbers
are taken to show, and in a few descriptions of code and papers.

**What John most needs to know:**

1. **Survey weights do not reach the quantile forests at all in a normal
   run.** Report 09 says survey weights "shape the trees" but are ignored when
   a leaf value is drawn. That is true only for a single-threaded fit.
   - `train_or_load_qrf()` passes `nthreads = n_threads()`, which is the Slurm
     CPU count. With more than one thread, `quantregForest` uses its own copy
     of the old forest code, `parallelRandomForest`. That copy has no
     `weights` argument and does not pass unknown arguments on, so the weights
     are dropped without a warning.
   - So the tips, overtime, auto-loan, childcare and mortgage-share forests
     are fitted unweighted in any multi-core run, under R 4.4.1 or 4.4.2.
   - This contradicts the repository's `CLAUDE.md`, which says the R 4.4.2
     rebuild "trains them weighted", and the comment in `tips.R`.
   - Both of those also say the older `randomForest` 4.7-1.1 lacked a weights
     argument. Its source has one; weights arrived in 4.7-1.
   - Report 09's plan to "weight the leaf draws" does not fix this on its own.
2. **`drf`'s `sample.weights` does nothing for Tax-Data's use.** Report 09
   reports this from project notes but did not test it. I settled it.
   - In the `drf` 1.3.1 source, the weights reach only the conditional-mean
     prediction. They do not reach the splitting rule, the tree subsampling or
     the donor weights.
   - A test fit with and without weights produced identical trees and
     identical donor weights.
   - The comment on `train_or_load_drf()` in `helpers.R`, which says the
     reverse, is wrong.
3. **Report 11's numbers reproduce.** Recomputed independently from the raw
   files:
   - mean wage per earner: +38.1% (CPS) and +38.6% (SSA);
   - SOI wages: +43.5%, and SOI returns: +6.7%;
   - the percentile growth rates;
   - the year-by-year gaps;
   - the jump in the largest released interest and dividend values.

   The years are aligned correctly. The not-in-universe codes (the IPUMS
   values that mean "this question does not apply to this person") are
   handled correctly for every variable used, and no real value was zeroed.
   Weights are used correctly.
4. **Report 11's "usable below the 90th percentile" does not come from its
   own check.** The check shows the CPS tracks SSA at the median, 75th, 90th
   and 95th percentiles, and misses at the 10th and the 25th.
   - At the 25th percentile, growth from 2015 to 2023 is +50.0% in the CPS
     against +57.9% in SSA: 8 points apart. The report omits the 25th
     percentile from its summary, and section 5 says the CPS holds "between
     about the 25th and 95th percentiles".
   - The 90th-percentile cut-off is borrowed from Realtime Inequality. That
     paper applies it to the monthly CPS because of top-coding, not to the
     ASEC.
5. **Most of the CPS's 2019 overshoot comes before the pandemic.**
   - With Census's nonresponse-adjusted weights (`ASECWTCVD`, on disk for
     income years 2019 and 2020, not used by report 11), the CPS's 2015–2019
     mean-wage growth is +16.1%, not +17.3%. The gap to SSA falls from 4.7 to
     3.5 points.
   - The CPS was already 2.8 to 3.1 points ahead in 2016–2018.
   - Mean wage growth across the 2019 processing change (income year 2017 to
     2018) was 3.5% in the CPS and 3.6% in SSA.
6. **Sampling noise is as large as the agreement the report relies on.** The
   CPS standard error of cumulative mean-wage growth is about 1.0 point to
   2019 and 1.2 points to 2023. At the 90th percentile it is about 2 to 3
   points. A proposed "drift" of 1 to 3 points a year therefore cannot be
   told apart from sampling error with this check.
7. **Report 11 describes the Realtime Inequality method wrongly in three
   places.**
   - It adds a re-sorting step the paper does not have.
   - It calls the overall wage level administrative. The paper averages QCEW
     and CPS predictions for the bottom 90%.
   - It applies the bottom-90% CPS rule to the ASEC. The paper applies it to
     the monthly CPS.
8. **The SSA-based recommendation (report 11's first) has a population gap
   the report does not mention.** SSA's wage distribution counts every W-2
   earner, including people who do not file and dependents. A PUF earner's
   percentile matches an SSA percentile only if the mapped population is the
   same: filers plus the non-filer pool, after the spouse split.
9. **Report 10's errors are about the state of other code.**
   - The CBO comparison is on GitHub `main` (pull request 127, 29 May 2026).
     Only the local `main` ref is stale.
   - A stale estate calibration warns; it does not stop the run.
   - Table 1.2 is on disk.
   - The 100–220 second GPU runtime is the model's own time. A Slurm job took
     about 5 to 15 minutes, and each Tax-Data vintage first needs a 7–11
     minute conversion.
   - The two-week build order assumes a new solver, a 2017-law itemization
     simulation and a converted candidate vintage, none of which exist.
10. **Report 09's inventory is accurate in substance, with four slips.**
    - Wealth uses 20 features, not 19.
    - Capital-gains basis ratios come from Sales of Capital Assets years 1985
      and 1997–2015, not a 2013–2015 panel.
    - Childcare is trained on the 2017 survey, which is income year 2016.
    - The sex imputation formula does not hit its DINA target in cells where
      sex is partly reported. The report says it does.
11. **`ipumsr` is not installed** anywhere this account's R can see. I checked
    the module libraries for R 4.4.1 and 4.4.2 and every user library. So
    `read_asec()` in `src/nonfilers/asec_tax_units.R` and
    `research/state_weights/nonfiler_pool/01_build_units.R` cannot run as the
    environment stands.
