# Step 30: the processing step run once on the 2016 SCF wave

The question: if the processing step agreed today is run once, outside
training, on the 2016 SCF wave, what does the filled-in SCF look like? The
step turns the SCF's reported interest, dividends and capital gains into
values on the PUF's concepts. Reporters keep the amounts they reported (the
dollar factor is 1 for all three items); only zero-reporters can change. A
"hidden holder" is a unit that reported zero on an item but is filled in with
a nonzero amount.

## What ran

One CPU job, `sbatch research/rebuild/minimal/30_run.sbatch process`
(script `30_process.py`), job 29977348. An earlier run of the same script,
job 29977289, gave the same numbers but lacked the comparison with the PUF
side at the SCF's mix of cells, so it was run again; its output was
overwritten. Outputs, in
`/nfs/roberts/scratch/pi_nrs36/jar335/minimal/checks/step30/`:
`process.md` (the tables below), `process.json` (cell targets, multipliers,
convergence) and `fills_2016.parquet` (one row per SCF unit row and item:
record id, household id, implicate, weight, cell, item, reported amount, five
filled-in amounts; 137,508 rows).

Data: table E27, tax year 2015, the 2016 SCF wave (34,377 unit rows, 6,248
households, five implicates, 139.6 million units) against the 2015 PUF plus
the crude non-filers (182.0 million units). Cells, size ranges, weights and
missing-value checks are those of `30_compare.py`, imported from it. The model
is step 29's C4 seed 1 (`model/step29/C4s1`: label on, table E27, rule 3
applied wave by wave).

The steps, for each item:

1. **Cell table.** In each cell and nonzero size range, the PUF side's
   weighted share of units in the range (p) and the SCF's (s). Where p > s,
   the target share of hidden holders is p − s; otherwise it is 0, the
   excess s − p is recorded, and no reporter is changed.
2. **Which zeros become holders.** For every SCF row that reported zero, the
   model's belief over the item's 84 slices, under the SCF label, with that
   item hidden and everything else on the record visible (net worth, wages,
   marital status, dependents, and the other two items at their reported
   values). The belief is collapsed to the size ranges plus zero. In each
   cell, one multiplier per range (and one for zero) is found by iterative
   proportional scaling so that the weighted sum, over the cell's
   zero-reporters, of each range's probability equals the target. One range
   (or zero) is drawn per row, five independent times.
3. **The amount.** A slice within the drawn range, from the belief
   restricted to that range; then a dollar amount drawn, with the PUF
   weights, from the PUF side's units in that slice and range (all cells
   pooled).

Choices made in carrying this out, beyond the spec:

- A slice can straddle a range edge (4 to 5 slices per item do; 58 for the
  before-limit capital gains, whose ranges are not on the line-13 slice
  edges). The collapse to ranges therefore uses the PUF side's composition
  of each slice: a slice's belief is split across ranges in proportion to
  the PUF-side weight of that slice in each range, and the amount is drawn
  from that slice's PUF-side units in the drawn range. Belief on slices with
  no PUF-side units cannot be placed in a range and is dropped before
  normalizing; it is 0.0012% of the belief for interest and less for the
  others.
- The amount pool is the whole PUF side (returns and crude non-filers), the
  same population the cell table uses.
- Capital gains before the loss limit (kg_st + kg_lt + kg_dist) uses the
  same line-13 belief, collapsed through the before-limit composition of
  each line-13 slice. It is a second version and has no "who" table.
- As in training, the model sees a reporter's capital loss beyond $3,000 as
  −$3,000; the reported amount itself is kept unchanged in the output.
- Simplification of training: each item is filled in conditioning on the
  other two items' *reported* values, not their fill-ins. In training the
  three are drawn in turn, each seeing the others' fill-ins.

The scaling matched every target: the largest miss in any cell is about
1e-10 of the cell's zeros. In the tables, "SCF filled in, expected" (from the
final probabilities) equals the PUF share in every range where the PUF
exceeds the SCF; the "five draws" column differs from it by sampling noise,
mostly under half a point.

## What it shows

**How many hidden holders.** Over all cells, 18.0% of SCF units are hidden
holders of interest (20.6% of the SCF's interest zero-reporters), 7.1% of
dividends (8.1% of zeros) and 8.6% of capital gains on the line-13 concept
(9.4% of zeros; 6.6% on the before-limit concept). The share holding
interest goes from 12.6% reported to 30.6% filled in; dividends from 12.0% to
19.2%; capital gains from 8.4% to 17.0%.

These filled-in shares are above the PUF side's overall shares (25.9%, 15.9%,
13.4%) for two reasons. First, the SCF's mix of units across cells differs
from the PUF side's: the PUF side has far more unmarried units with low wages,
who rarely hold these items. At the SCF's cell mix, the PUF side's holding
shares are 30.0%, 18.7% and 15.9%. Second, the excesses are left in place:
where SCF reporters exceed the PUF in a range, nothing is taken away, so the
filled-in shares end above the PUF at the SCF's mix by the excess (0.6, 0.5
and 1.0 points).

**Who the hidden holders are (interest, all cells).** They sit between the
reporters and the remaining zeros on every measure:

| share of SCF units | 12.6% | 18.0% | 69.4% |
| net worth, median | $739,000 | $326,000 | $26,000 |

The same ordering holds for dividends and capital gains over all cells and
in most single cells: the model picks as hidden holders the zero-reporters
with more wealth and with other investment income. The exceptions are in the
thinner unmarried cells. For interest among unmarried units with wages under
$25,000, hidden holders' median net worth ($155,000) is above the reporters'
($41,000); for dividends among unmarried units with wages of $25,000 to
$75,000, hidden holders have higher mean and median net worth than reporters;
for capital gains among unmarried units with wages under $25,000, 27% of
hidden holders report interest against 9% of reporters.

**Dollars.** Hidden holders are mostly small amounts, so they add less to
dollars than to counts, but they still push the SCF totals above the PUF:

In totals: interest $89.9 billion reported, $105.2 billion filled in, $94.2
billion on the PUF side (whose population is 182 million units against the
SCF's 140 million); dividends $249.9, $276.4 and $246.2 billion; capital gains
$678.3, $697.5 and $626.5 billion.

**Things that look wrong or need a decision.**

1. With reporters' dollars kept, SCF reporters alone already give about the
   PUF's interest and dividends per unit at the same cell mix ($644 against
   $653; $1,789 against $1,721). With hidden holders added, the filled-in SCF
   is 15% above the PUF at that mix for interest, 15% for dividends and 9%
   for capital gains. Either the SCF's reported dollars run
   above the PUF's for those who report, or the two sources' cell
   populations differ in ways the cells do not capture; this run cannot
   tell which.
2. In the married, wages over $200,000 cell the targets are large: 74% of
   interest zeros, 42% of dividend zeros and 51% of capital gains zeros
   become holders. The excesses there are also the largest (9.7, 6.7 and 9.2
   points), so the holding share ends well above the PUF's (86% against 77%
   for interest).
3. Capital gains losses grow: $21.8 billion reported, $34.8 billion filled
   in, $18.9 billion on the PUF side (line 13). Hidden holders add $13.0
   billion of losses, and SCF reporters with losses beyond $3,000 (0.5% of
   units overall), which line 13 cannot show, are kept as reported. In three
   cells (unmarried with wages of $25,000 and over, both cells, and married
   with wages over $200,000) the hidden holders' mean capital gain is
   negative.
4. Small positive differences at the top of thin cells produce a few very
   large fill-ins. For example, in the cell of unmarried units with wages
   under $25,000, 0.02% of units become hidden holders of capital gains of
   $250,000 and over, with a mean drawn amount of $1.2 million. Such
   differences are partly sampling noise in the cell table.
5. In the higher-wage cells, interest hidden holders hold very little (mean
   $63 to $116), so most of them are holders of under $100. In the cell of
   unmarried units with wages under $25,000, hidden holders' mean interest
   ($1,745) is above the reporters' ($401), because the PUF side has more
   holders of $500 and over there than the SCF reports.
