# Shared X across tax data and surveys

P has X and Z; S has X and Y. The model borrows Y onto P records through X. A shared band must therefore describe comparable units in both sources. A matching marginal distribution is neither necessary nor sufficient: populations can differ for legitimate reasons, and equal marginals can hide different conditional relationships with Y and Z. The useful target is a common *measurement of the same concept on the same population*, followed by checks of the joint distribution of shared variables.

## A. Why two versions of X differ

In the table, “detect” means distinguish the stated cause from competing causes using only the two weighted marginals and outside totals such as SOI. A visible distributional feature does not identify its cause. SOI is an authority for a tax concept and a return population; it is not an authority for a survey's broader concept or household population. “Top” includes the level and composition of the upper tail, not just its share of dollars.

| Mechanism | Effect on S relative to P | Detect from marginals and totals alone? | Example or pertinent evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Population coverage and filing selection | Nonfilers, institutions, or excluded households change zero mass and the positive mix; dollars and top shares change through selection even if each response is exact. | No. Counts reveal a coverage gap, but do not identify which X values the extra units would have on a return. | The PUF represents filers; SCF and CPS include nonfilers. Zero wages are 30.45% in SCF versus 17.14% in PUF. The CPS build has 166.9 million nondependent tax units against 150.8 million PUF returns. |
| Unit construction and within-unit aggregation | One household may contain multiple tax units. Combining wages or interest changes holder shares, positive ranks, and the top; different dependent and marital rules also move zeros. | No. Unit counts help, but the joint membership and within-household amounts are missing. | CPS constructs tax units from households, keeps dependent-headed units, and makes every married couple joint; PUF includes separate filers. SCF household units need the same alignment. |
| Survey design, nonresponse, and weights | Different inclusion probabilities or response propensities change weighted zeros, ranks, and top estimates without any changed reported dollar on a sampled unit. | Sometimes a large discontinuity or sensitivity to weights is visible; the cause and correction are not identified. | SCF oversamples wealthy families; PUF has its own stratification. Both require their survey weights for marginal comparisons. |
| Tax versus survey definition | Adding untaxed components or omitting taxable components changes holder status, positive shape, totals, and possibly the top. The difference need not be a scale factor. | No; documentation or component data are needed. | CPS `INCINT` includes IRA interest and totals 2.29 times SOI taxable interest. Taxable interest in PUF is a narrower concept. |
| Realization, netting, and sign convention | Gross receipts, realized net gains, distributions, deductible losses, and survey gains occupy different zero and loss cells; positive ranks and top can move in either direction. | Signs and mass at legal limits are clues, not an attribution. | PUF Schedule D gains/losses are not automatically SCF gains. PUF has 5.58% net capital losses versus SCF's 1.77%; the 2015 detection table uses absolute amounts for brackets, which hides this sign difference unless checked separately. |
| Other income concepts and taxable reporting rules | Business, farm, pension, and wage categories can overlap or exclude different components. A concept added to wages can create new holders and move the upper tail. | No; component definitions or linked records are needed. | In the controlled D3 experiment, adding SCF business and farm income to wages raised the wage total from $7.67T to $9.40T and changed the relation to wealth. |
| Small-amount omission and item nonresponse | Amount-dependent failure to report moves small positives to zero or missing, raises the observed positive ranks, and may leave the reported-dollar mean within brackets nearly unchanged; some rich holders may also be missing. | A low-end deficit is visible, but omission cannot be separated from real nonownership or coverage by marginals alone. | SCF interest zeros are 87.35% versus PUF 71.67%; among reported $1–$100 interest, weighted means are $33 in both sources. This is consistent with missed small amounts, not proof of it. |
| Imputation, proxy response, recall, and heaping | Imputation can smooth or concentrate values; proxies and recall can misstate both participation and levels. Rounding creates point masses and can turn small values into zero. Top values can also move. | Heaps are detectable; their source and the latent values are not. | Controlled D4 rounding to $1,000 removes SCF amounts from $1–$500 and cuts measured interest holders from 12.6% to 5.8%. Survey rounding is plausible in CPS, but was not checked in the CPS scoping note. |
| Global or amount-dependent dollar error | A scale changes positive dollar cutoffs and totals but, if applied to all true nonzeros without rounding, preserves zeros, sign, and ranks. A nonlinear error also changes positive ranks relative to P and can distort the top. | A proportional shift can be diagnosed under common population, concept, and holder assumptions; it is not uniquely identified without them. | Controlled D1 halves all SCF shared amounts: own-source bands barely change, PUF cuts fail, and rescaling before PUF cuts restores the bridge. |
| Top-coding, swapping, censoring, and agency editing | A cap or replacement creates a pileup or a missing tail; positive top ranks are tied or scrambled and totals fall. Bottom edits can make small amounts disappear. | A visible cap or heap is detectable, but the lost conditional tail is not. | CPS wage, interest, and dividend extracts have top-code flags. Controlled D5 caps SCF values at PUF p99 and makes within-source ranks especially poor at the top. The PUF's four omitted aggregate top records also limit what its released tail can teach. |
| Timing within the income concept | Survey interview date, reference-year wording, realized-versus-accrued income, late payments, and return amendments move dollars across years; zeros and top gains can change sharply. | No, unless source timing and repeated observations are supplied. | SCF waves refer to income years 2015, 2018, and 2021, whereas CPS ASEC 2016 reports income year 2015. Matching by survey label instead of income year would misalign X. |
| It is a different year | Inflation and nominal growth move dollar cutoffs; business cycles, asset returns, tax policy, and filing behavior also change zeros, positive ranks, and the top. Tax law can change the *reported concept* itself. | Year labels and outside yearly totals establish that the years differ; totals cannot separate price growth from compositional or legal change. | IRS 2021/2015 capital-gain totals grow 2.911 times, SCF totals 2.598 times. The 2018 tax law is a separate hazard for taxable items and deductions. |
| Data processing and missing-variable support | Agency allocation, suppression, sampling, and a question absent in one year create structural missingness. Coding an absent question as zero invents nonholders; tails may be unobserved. | Metadata and flags identify absence; marginals alone cannot tell a structural missing value from zero. | CPS capital gains were not asked before income year 2018. The PUF top aggregate records cannot be treated as ordinary tax units. |

The mechanisms interact. For instance, an apparent scale difference can be produced by an income definition, a shifted population, or a top-code. A low-end reporting curve estimated as the SCF/PUF bracket ratio also absorbs the extra SCF nonfilers unless populations have first been aligned. The detection table's good within-bracket means for interest and dividends establish the amounts *among reporters in broad brackets*; they do not establish the latent amounts of nonreporters or equality at the top.

## B. Available normalizations

Each operation below applies to S's X or to its representation before training, except where stated. A “fix” here is conditional on the assumption in the last column. For gains, treat negative amounts separately from positive amounts; the 10 loss bands and the tax loss limit cannot be inferred from positive percentiles.

| Operation | Corrects; leaves or worsens | Assumption and behavior across years |
|---|---|---|
| Rank S within its own year among positives, with zero separate; retain 73 positive bins | Removes monotone dollar growth and global scaling among reporters. Leaves the holder gap and maps the lowest SCF holder to the lowest PUF holder even when SCF misses small holders. Top-code ties destroy top order. | Positive percentile means the same economic position among *reporters*. Easy to pool years, but E0/Y0 shows the wealth error and later-year PUF holder drift. |
| Rank over the full weighted distribution, counting zeros in the percentile coordinate; preserve an explicit zero indicator | Places an SCF positive at roughly the 87th percentile or above and a PUF positive at roughly the 72nd or above in the interest example. This absorbs much of the rank misalignment from unequal holder shares without dollar cuts. It does not recover missing holders or make a zero mean the same thing; a single zero token still has source-dependent mass. | Assumes a percentile of the full, aligned population has comparable economic meaning. Recompute by year. With nonfilers or real participation changes, it can move the wrong people together; ties at zero need an explicit rule, never random dollar imputation. This is a proposed representation, not a grid result. |
| Band S on P's weighted dollar cuts, including zero and separate loss cuts | Gives equal dollar amounts equal X tokens despite different holder shares; keeps P's support and holder rate available for P-style records. Does not repair concepts, coverage, rounding, or unknown tails. | Assumes same dollar means comparable X. E4 reduced PUF-style wealth total from 1.92 to 1.11 times the SCF total, with holder shares intact. Requires P cuts or a defensible projection each year. |
| Translate S dollars by an audited constant or monotone quantile map above a reliable line, then apply P cuts | Repairs a genuine scale shift, or a verified amount-dependent transformation. Leaves zero/coverage gaps; an unrestricted quantile map can conceal different concepts and erase genuine distributional change. | Needs a common population, concept, and adequate overlap. Estimate a factor from matched positive quantiles or external component totals, not the unadjusted S/P total when holder rates differ. D1 translation worked; D2 missed 10% wage scaling and upward gains scaling; D3 could not be fixed by scaling. Re-estimate or validate by year. |
| Use an amount-specific reporting probability for S; integrate latent X in the model's loss | In principle repairs stochastic omission of true holders and preserves P's small values. Leaves structural nonfilers, definition errors, and nonignorable missingness. The latent tail is weakly identified. | Requires a measured reporting curve conditional on other shared variables and a constraint that prevents the model from routing P and S through separate source paths. E9 improved on E7 but left the median about three times the SCF's; it is not an established fix. Curves can change by wave. |
| Flip selected S zeros into holders, drawing amounts from a P-based conditional model | Repairs a known missing-holder mechanism if the chosen units are the omitted holders. Can invent taxable income for true nonholders and distort Y given X or the top. | Requires external participation counts and a credible selection model, preferably validation data. E6 improved mean wealth but raised the top 1% share to 50.3% versus 39.7%. Refit selection by year. |
| Coarsen both sources to their common reliable resolution: threshold, broad brackets, or top-code; keep fine P detail as P-owned | Repairs small-amount omission, heaping, or censoring for the *shared* slot while retaining a way to generate fine P amounts. Loses bridge information below the threshold or above a cap and can mix unlike concepts even in a coarse cell. | Requires a reliability limit and a model for P-only residuals. E1's simple zeroing reduced wealth error but cut P holder rates to 12/11/8%; E2's split retained holders but only partly repaired wealth. Year-specific limits may be nominal or indexed according to survey practice. |
| Restrict or reweight S to the P target population; construct comparable tax units and filing status | Repairs coverage, unit and some weighting differences. May discard information about nonfilers and changes Y's target population; filing classification errors remain. | Needs household structure and a filing model or known filing status. Keep nonfilers as a separate population with a filing flag if the final population includes them, as the design plan specifies. Update filing rules and weights by year. |
| Use an explicit source token or source-specific observation layer while constraining a common latent X | Can represent known reporting processes and source-owned Y/Z. An unconstrained token lets the network learn incompatible X meanings and hide a bad bridge. | Requires identified measurement rules and shared-variable label-indifference checks. Removing the label alone failed in E7–E9; keeping it alone is no correction. Year interactions must be constrained or checked. |
| Propagate P's base-year cuts using external yearly growth, or use a calibrated rank-to-dollar map | Provides a common nominal reference when later P microdata do not exist. A single total-growth factor misses changing inequality, participation, tax-law definitions, and losses. SCF percentile growth can carry its own reporting drift into the reference. | Needs year-specific SOI counts/totals and stable conditional measurement. Y1, using IRS total growth, had PUF-style wealth means 0.96, 1.04, 1.01 times the same-year SCF means; the later-year medians remained high. Y1/Y2/Y3 differences near 10% are unsettled in one-seed runs. |
| Correct generated records with entropy tilt to SOI and survey targets | Hits known counts and totals and can repair some aggregate output errors. Cannot identify a missing conditional Y–Z link; large or concentrated changes can harm untargeted groups and the top. | Needs compatible, correctly defined yearly targets. E11 met wealth targets but lowered P holder shares; E6 had a cheap tilt despite an excessive top. Track group errors, top quantiles, and effective sample size as well as the move in nats. |
| Remove a variable from the shared bridge; retain source-specific versions or components | Prevents a known incompatible concept from supplying false evidence. Weakens the bridge and leaves its cross-source association unidentified unless other X variables carry it. | Appropriate for CPS interest until taxable components can be separated, and for CPS capital gains before 2018 because the question is absent. Source-specific definitions and availability can vary by year. |

## C. Choice rule and its limits

| Diagnosed mechanism | First operation; additional check |
|---|---|
| Different population, unit, or weights | Align tax units, filing population, and weights before judging the item; compare counts and X jointly with filing status. |
| Same concept and holders, genuine level shift | Translate dollars above a reliable line, then common P cuts; verify several positive quantiles and top totals. |
| Missed small holders or rounding | Common-resolution split or verified reporting model, then common cuts; inspect P holder rates and Y by other X. Full-distribution ranks are a useful sensitivity case. |
| Definition, sign, or timing difference | Reconstruct a common tax concept from components, or make the variable source-owned; check legal and survey documentation. |
| Top-code or PUF aggregate top | Coarsen only the shared top and use external top targets or a separately identified tail; do not infer individual top order from a cap. |
| Different year | Align income years and policy definitions; project cuts or use a calibrated rank-dollar map with year-specific participation and totals. |
| Residual aggregate mismatch after a sound bridge | Tilt to compatible targets; inspect conditional and top errors, not only target fit. |

A practical rule for each X is:

1. Define the target unit, filing population, income year, tax concept, and sign. Mark structural absence, top-coding, and edited cells from documentation. If a common concept cannot be constructed, do not call the item shared.
2. With appropriate weights and aligned populations, compare zero and loss shares, positive bracket counts, within-bracket means, weighted quantiles, heaps/caps, and top totals. Compare SOI counts and totals for the *same tax concept and returns*. Repeat by other shared X and filing status; these conditional comparisons are needed for a bridge.
3. Choose the narrowest justified repair: translate a documented dollar shift; coarsen a documented resolution limit; model reporting only with evidence for its selection rule; otherwise use common dollar cuts as a working reference and flag the unresolved mechanism. Do not force an S total to SOI if S covers more people or a broader concept.
4. For a year without P, project the reference using annual SOI holder counts and totals where possible, plus an explicit assumption about positive shape. Validate against held-out survey waves and any later P or tax tables. Apply the year-specific rank-to-dollar map after generation and tilt to compatible targets.
5. Reject a repair if it makes the same-record prediction depend strongly on the source label, shifts the P holder rates, or changes Y by wage/interest groups or top wealth in an implausible way. A small entropy move alone is not sufficient.

There is a precise identification limit. Let observed S positive amounts have density `f_S(x)`. They can arise from true P-like amounts transformed by `x = a v`, giving `f_S(x) = f_P(x/a)/a` among comparable holders. They can also arise from selecting holders with a value-dependent probability `q(v)` and renormalizing, giving `f_S(v) ∝ q(v) f_P(v)`. Suitable `q` can mimic many transformed positive distributions over the observed range. In the strict special case of a common population, exact zeros, and pure scaling of every nonzero amount, scaling preserves the holder share while omitted holders raise zeros; zero mass then separates the two. In actual PUF–survey comparisons, nonfilers, unit construction, rounded zeros, imputation, and definition changes remove those assumptions. Even equal zero shares do not establish pure scaling: offsetting selection and false positives can produce them. SOI totals constrain the tax-return distribution but add no information about which survey zero was a latent holder or which survey dollars correspond to taxable dollars. The tie is broken by matched records, survey components and questionnaires, administrative validation, repeated measurements, top-code flags, or credible restrictions on reporting probability and population membership. Conditional distributions can reject a simple hypothesis, but do not by themselves identify the true one.

## D. Years without P microdata

There is a representation that pools years without *contemporaneous* P cuts, but it cannot dispense with an anchor and assumptions. Use a base-year P reference CDF for each shared tax concept. Give each record its zero/loss indicator and its position on a common full-population percentile scale, with the 84-band layout finer at the top; keep the year token. Project the base reference to year `t` using available SOI participation counts and positive-dollar totals, with separate treatment of the positive shape and legal loss limits. A projected rank-to-dollar map supplies dollars after generation. The model sees comparable positions across years only if filing population, tax concept, reporting process, and the relation of Y to those positions are stable or explicitly modeled by year. The yearly map carries nominal growth; the year token can carry association drift. Annual SOI aggregate growth alone does not determine the map's quantiles.

Pure within-source, within-year ranks also pool without P cuts, and full-distribution ranks absorb much of a holder-share gap. They do so by assuming that the same population percentile identifies a comparable tax unit in P and S. The interest example violates that assumption if SCF's extra zeros are unreported small taxable amounts; it also violates it in another way if they are genuine nonfilers. A single zero bin cannot encode where each latent holder belongs. Fixed P cuts grown by SOI totals are the more defensible current working rule: Y1 repaired the broad wealth level in all three waves, whereas own-source cuts Y0 kept means 37%–61% too high. Neither rule identifies later-year P-style holder rates or the top from one 2015 PUF. The 2021 gains surge, 2018 law changes, CPS definition differences, and changing survey reporting can all break an extrapolated map. Year-specific SOI counts and bracket totals, component definitions, and held-out waves are needed to test where it breaks; a year token alone cannot supply those facts.
